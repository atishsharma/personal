CORE

Reference Manual

CORE stores programs at a PAGE-KEY location: 16 pages × 16 keys (0-9, a-F), plus the three direct page keys, 259 in all. It ships with the factory presets loaded: Sony on pages 1-6, Mitsubishi on 7, 8, 9 and E, Zenith on F and b.

  1. P 8 shows 8-. Press a to send TV Power: the display reads 8-a with a C on the right while it transmits.
  2. Once on a page, a single key runs that location.
  3. a- b- c- jump straight to pages a, b and c, and can hold a program of their own.
  4. (KEY) then a key selects it without running it (0-o).
  5. CANCEL stops a running program. Press P twice to wake the display without sending anything.
  1. Open the programming cover.
  2. Choose the location: P 0 (KEY) a shows 0-a.
  3. ENABLE: En appears at the right of the status line.
  4. DO: the location moves to the top and the status line shows L-.
  5. Send the signal with the IR Signal Simulator. You get C and a chime (Ɔ for a one-shot code). Up to six codes per key.
  6. OK: a chime, a brief J, and it is stored. Turn ENABLE off and press a to test.

DO then OK with nothing in between empties a location.

Put VCR Power, TV Power and VCR Play on one key (manual p.12):

  1. P 0 (KEY) b, ENABLE, DO.
  2. P E a, P 8 a, P 9 b. The status line fills: LPE-aP8-a-, then scrolls with ←.
  3. DELETE removes the last keystroke. OK saves.

Repeat loop: → count 1-99 P page key ←. For example → 5 P 1 d ← turns the volume up five steps.

Redefine a key from a program: (KEY) key DO … OK inside a program.

Programs hold up to 62 keystrokes. Too many, or more than six codes, gives two buzzes. A program that calls itself is stopped with two buzzes. CORE checks the syntax on OK and buzzes if a step is incomplete.

Absolute (AT), 3 minutes from now (p.15):

  1. ENABLE, AT: A- on the status line, AT beside the time.
  2. Set the time: digits, or a/b hour, c/d minute, E/F day (MF = Monday-Friday), a-/b- AM/PM. Options: P RPT (every week), c- 2nd (second occurrence). Here: d d d.
  3. DO, then the location: P 0 b, then OK.

Relative (WAIT): ENABLE WAIT, digits M S S (or a first for H M M), DO, location, OK. WAIT 0 1 5 is 15 seconds.

Event queue: EDIT AT shows the next timer. ←/→ browse, DELETE (with En on) removes it, OK leaves.

A timer sounds three chimes, flashes the bell and runs the location (an empty location just sounds the alarm). Inside a program, WAIT … DO location OK pauses before running that location. Minutes wrap without changing the hour, as on the real unit.

  1. SET: ≡ appears on the status line.
  2. Direct digits: 7 3 2 gives 7:00, 7:30, 7:32. Times from 10:00 to 12:59 need four digits.
  3. Or scan with the rockers: a/b hour, c/d minute.
  4. a- AM, b- PM, E/F day. OK sets it.

Digits reset the seconds to :00. Rockers alone keep them. The clock starts on your computer's time and keeps your setting.

  1. EDIT, then the location (P 0 b). The program shows with a blinking cursor and EDIT lit.
  2. ←/→ move the cursor. Arrows on the display mean there is more off-screen.
  3. With En on: DELETE removes the keystroke left of the cursor, and any other key is inserted.
  4. EDIT saves. CANCEL discards the changes.

The reset hole sits between (KEY) and DO. A reset restarts CORE and keeps your programs. Within 4 seconds after it:

  1. a- clears the event queue.
  2. c (MIN rocker, left) clears the queue and every program, factory presets included.

Tones: one chime = accepted. One buzz = rejected. Two buzzes = memory full or recursion. Two chimes = a timer is due while you review the queue. Three chimes = a timer is running.
